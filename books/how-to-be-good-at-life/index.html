How to Be Good at Life

Simple systems to achieve more and stress less.

by Ben Meer · 2026

Share on X

Ben Meer, who writes the System Sunday newsletter, starts from a simple observation: the people who keep getting things done don't have more willpower or more hours than everyone else. They have better systems. Discipline runs out and motivation comes and goes. A good system keeps working on the days you don't feel like it, and it takes a lot of the stress out of your day as well.

The core of the book is DRIVE, a five-step method for turning a goal into a system you can actually run. Meer then applies it across seven "Life Engines": health, relationships, career, money, emotions, mind and environment. Along the way there are more than 40 ready-made systems, including the weekly System Sunday review, plus short case studies of how people like Jerry Seinfeld, Serena Williams, Hans Zimmer and Andy Warhol organised their work.

This is the same idea as James Clear's "you fall to the level of your systems", but taken beyond habits to cover every part of a life. Some of it will feel familiar if you read a lot in this area. Its strength is that it is practical: you can pick one system and start using it this week.

Key ideas

  • Lasting results come from systems, not from extra willpower or extra time.
  • DRIVE is a five-step method for turning any goal into a system that runs on its own.
  • Your life runs on seven engines (health, relationships, career, money, emotions, mind, environment), and each one needs its own systems.
  • A weekly review, "System Sunday", keeps the whole setup in shape.
  • Good systems should make life calmer and easier, not just more productive.
Worth it if

you set goals that fade after a few weeks and want a practical toolkit for building routines that last, rather than another pep talk about discipline.
