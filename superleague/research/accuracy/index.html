How accurate are football predictions?

An honest answer, from this site's own Super League model. Over 517 games since 2024-25, the market's closing odds predicted results more accurately than the model. The model is more useful for match stats such as shots and corners, where it clearly beats a league average. Its 55 published picks have won 38 and lost 17, but 35 of the 40 with a closing price were taken at a worse price than the close, so that record proves little so far.

Three ways to judge a prediction

  • Does it beat a simple average? A prediction is only useful if it does better than the obvious guess. For a match stat, the obvious guess is the league average.
  • Are its chances honest? When a model says 60%, it should happen about 60% of the time over many games. A model can pick plenty of winners and still be over-confident.
  • Does it beat the market? The closing odds just before kick-off carry everything the market knows, from team news to late money. Beating them is the hardest test, and the one that decides whether a prediction is worth betting on.

Both scores punish a confident wrong call more than a cautious one. The gaps look small, but they favour the market on both questions, and the two picked a winning favourite equally often. In plain terms: on who wins and on whether there are three goals, the closing odds know a little more than the model.

Betting the disagreements says the same, only less clearly. Backing every outcome where the model's chance times the average closing price was at least 1.05 returned −5.2% on home, draw or away (346 bets) and +6.6% on over/under 2.5 (178 bets). The 95% ranges are wide, −27.7% to +17.3% and −9.1% to +22.3%, so neither result shows an edge, or rules one out.

The model does best where the teams drive the number: shots most of all, then shots on target, corners and goals. Cards and fouls barely move off the average; they depend more on the referee and the course of the game than on the two teams. The site's picks are on these match stats.

The picks: a good record that proves little yet

The site publishes a pick when the model's chance times the market's price is at least 1.10. Since the start: 55 picks, 38 won, 17 lost, +30.9% on flat stakes. The model gave its picks 64.4% on average, and 69.1% won.

The closing price tells a different story. Of the 40 picks with a closing price, only 5 were taken at a better price than the close; on average the price was 6.6% worse (95% range 5.1% to 8.1% worse). When the market moves against nearly every pick, a winning record usually means the picks have been lucky, and the next hundred are likely to do worse. Over a few dozen picks, chance alone can move the return by 20 points or more either way.

Every pick, with the time it was first published, is on the track record.

What to ask of any football prediction

  1. Compared with what? A record with no comparison to the closing odds says little about skill.
  2. Are the chances honest? Ask for a table of stated chance against what happened.
  3. How many? Under a few hundred picks, luck dominates the result.
  4. Published when? Picks should carry a time from before kick-off that anyone can check.

Limits

  • Results are compared on league games from 2024-25 to 18 October 2026 that have closing odds in the public football-data.co.uk archive.
  • The model turns its expected goals into chances with a standard Poisson model; the Big 4 correction and the settings for each stat were fitted on seasons that include these games.
  • The betting test uses average closing prices; a bettor shopping for the best price would do somewhat better on every line.
