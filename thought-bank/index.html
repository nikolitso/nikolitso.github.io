      Physical Health

      Move, sleep, eat well: the body carries everything else.

      The older I get, the more I realize you can get pretty damn far in life by just doing what most people avoid. Wake up early. Focus deeply. Move your body. Eat real foods. Obsess over one thing. Have difficult conversations. Slow down. Be present. It's the recipe for a good life.

      Research shows that a quick, phone-free walk boosts focus, clears your mind, and sharpens your thinking. In one study, kids who took a 20-minute walk before a test scored higher in comprehension, spelling, and math—proof that even a little movement goes a long way for our brains.

      The power of a 20-minute walk

      Going from zero exercise to just 90 minutes a week cuts your risk of dying from all causes by 14%. As Peter Attia puts it, it's very hard to find any drug that can do that.

      Peter Attia, Exercise and Mortality

      Unhappy People vs Happy People

      Happiness comes from solving problems. The keyword here is "solving." If you're avoiding your problems or feel like you don't have any problems, then you're going to make yourself miserable. If you feel like you have problems that you can't solve, you will likewise make yourself miserable. The secret sauce is in the solving of the problems, not in not having problems in the first place.

      To be happy we need something to solve. Happiness is therefore a form of action; it's an activity, not something that is passively bestowed upon you, not something that you magically discover in a top-ten article on the Huffington Post or from any specific guru or teacher. It doesn't magically appear when you finally make enough money to add on that extra room to the house. You don't find it waiting for you in a place, an idea, a job—or even a book, for that matter.

      Happiness is a constant work-in-progress, because solving problems is a constant work-in-progress—the solutions to today's problems will lay the foundation for tomorrow's.

      Happiness is a skill:

      - The first step is realizing you can raise your happiness, and that more of it is under your control than you assume. - Happiness is a skill you develop and a choice you make. You decide it matters, work at it, and slowly raise your baseline, just as you would with fitness.

      Desire less:

      - Desire is a contract you make to be unhappy until you get what you want. You work hard, you're miserable in the meantime, and when you get it you go back to where you were. - No single thing will make you happy forever. That's a delusion. If obtaining things made us permanently happy, we'd all be happier than our ancestors — and we're not. - Happiness is returning to the state where nothing is missing in this moment. - When you catch yourself wanting something, ask: is this so important I'd be unhappy unless it goes my way? Usually not.

      Seek peace:

      - A lot of what we call happiness is just pleasure. True happiness comes out of peace, and peace comes from understanding yourself — and from being careful about desire, judgment, and reaction. - Peace is happiness at rest; happiness is peace in motion. The real goal is peace. - What you want is peace from the mind itself, not just peace of mind. Your inner voice goes quiet at the height of pleasure. - Stress is wanting two incompatible things at once. Accept what's outside your control, and the stress disappears.

      Understand, don't fight yourself:

      - You cannot work toward peace directly. Work toward understanding, and peace follows. - The search for peace is the search for truth. See a habit clearly enough, and it can dissolve on its own. - Self-improvement is often just dressed-up self-conflict. Use curiosity to understand things, and you'll change naturally.

      Don't look outside:

      - Happiness is internal. All real success is internal, and has very little to do with external circumstances. - Money solves money problems, but it won't make you a different person. A fit body, a calm mind, and a house full of love cannot be bought — they must be earned. - Being unhappy is extremely inefficient — a peaceful mind makes better long-term decisions.

      Train the baseline:

      - Meditate to see how your mind works, then stay aware in the moment. If you catch yourself judging, look for the positive. - Peace of body makes peace of mind easier. Work out every day, get sunlight, and drop what destabilizes you. - Don't hang around people who constantly pick fights. If they're always fighting someone else, they'll eventually fight you. - Choose your five closest people carefully. Like chimps, you can be predicted by the five you hang out with most. - Busy is the death of productivity and happiness. Leave empty space in the week; good ideas often arrive only after boredom sets in.

      Don't take it too seriously:

      - Don't take yourself so seriously. You're barely here — a firefly blinking once in the night. This life is short and precious, so don't spend most of it being unhappy. - Focus on one or two things that really matter, and surrender the rest. Accept it as it comes, and be glad you're here.

      Naval Ravikant, How to Be Happy

      Money
