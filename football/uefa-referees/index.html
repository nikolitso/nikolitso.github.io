Methodology

Which games

The Champions League, Europa League and Conference League from 2024–25 on, league phase onwards: the knockout play-offs and every knockout round, no qualifying rounds. Only the referee in charge counts; assistants, fourth officials and VAR are not credited with cards.

How cards are counted

As bookmakers settle card markets: a yellow card counts 1 and a red card 2. A second yellow is ignored and only its red counts, so one player can add at most 3 (yellow 1 + red 2). Cards shown to non-players do not count: managers and staff, substitutes who have not come on, and players already substituted off. Whether a carded player was on the pitch is checked against the lineups and the exact time of each substitution. Only the scheduled 90 minutes count, stoppage time included: cards shown after the full-time whistle, in extra time or in a penalty shoot-out are left out. In these 1314 games, 276 cards to non-players were left out and 25 games had cards in extra time or a shoot-out that were left out.

The card index

Referees do not choose their games. A Conference League referee and a Champions League referee face different matches, so each referee's games are compared with the average game in the same competition and season. The index is the referee's cards divided by that average, after adding 10 average games to every record: a referee with few games sits close to 1.00 until their own games say otherwise. Above 1.00 means more cards than expected, below means fewer.

How evenly matched the teams were

Each club gets a rating built only from UEFA results before the game, so no game is rated with hindsight. Every club starts from a level set by the competition it first appears in, gains or loses rating with each result (more for a bigger winning margin), and the home side gets a small edge, except in a final. The favourite's chance is its expected score from those ratings, a draw counting half: under 60% is an even game, 80% or more a big mismatch. Early in a club's record the rating is rough, so read the bands as broad groups.

Two-legged ties

The two games of a knockout tie are paired, and each second leg is grouped by the first-leg score from the second-leg hosts' side: leading, level or trailing. Finals and league-phase games are single games.

Reading the numbers

One referee's average carries a lot of noise. Each profile shows a 90% range for the referee's true average and a sample label; with fewer than 10 games treat everything as a first impression. These are descriptive statistics, not predictions: the games a referee is given, the teams and what is at stake all shape the cards.

Data

Match data from the Opta feed: referee, every card with its type, minute and player, substitutions, lineups and fouls. Referee appointments appear when UEFA announces them, usually two or three days before kick-off. Fouls are the fouls committed by both teams. A referee's country is the football association they referee for, which is not in the feed and is added separately.
