Nine Things Successful People Do Differently

The specific habits that separate people who actually reach their goals.

by Heidi Grant Halvorson · 2017

Heidi Grant Halvorson’s widely-cited short piece (this is the expanded book version) distills goal-achievement research into nine specific, concrete behaviors — get specific about your goal, seize the moment to act, know exactly how far you have left to go, be a realistic optimist, focus on getting better rather than being good, and a few more in that vein.

The 'be a realistic optimist' principle is the one that does the most quiet correcting of conventional wisdom — Halvorson's research finds that people who only visualize success without also rehearsing the specific obstacles in the way actually perform worse than people who plan for friction, which cuts directly against the more feel-good, pure-visualization advice common in the genre.

Key ideas

  • The book distills goal-achievement research into nine specific, concrete behaviors — get specific, seize the moment, know how far you have left to go.
  • Imagining success without also planning for the obstacles in the way makes you less likely to reach it, not more.
  • Being a "realistic optimist" — rehearsing specific obstacles, not just visualizing success — predicts better performance than pure positive visualization.
  • It's the more actionable companion to Succeed — closer to a checklist you can apply to your next goal this week.
Worth it if

you want the practical, checklist version of Halvorson’s research rather than the fuller theoretical treatment.
