Interception

How syndicates and algorithms quietly run the modern sports betting market.

by Ed Miller, Matthew Davidow · 2023

Share on X

Miller, a professional poker player and betting theorist, and Davidow pull back the curtain on how the modern sports betting industry actually works — not from the bettor's side, but from inside the machinery of lines, syndicates, and the algorithms that set and move odds.

The thesis is unsettling for casual bettors: the market is far more efficient and adversarial than most people assume, dominated by syndicates and bots that recreational bettors are unknowingly betting against.

It's less a "how to win" manual than an exposé of market structure, which makes it more valuable for understanding why beating the books is hard than for extracting a specific edge — though the authors keep technical concepts grounded in concrete industry examples.

The most valuable chapters walk through specific mechanics recreational bettors rarely think about — how a book's initial line gets set before any public money has moved, how syndicate action gets detected and limited, and why a bettor who's actually beating the closing line consistently gets treated very differently by a book than one who isn't. That operational detail is what separates this from the more motivational betting-strategy books on the shelf.

Key ideas

  • The sports betting market is far more efficient and adversarial than most casual bettors assume, dominated by syndicates and bots.
  • The line you're betting against was already shaped by sharper money before you ever saw it.
  • A bettor who's actually beating the closing line consistently gets treated very differently by a book than one who isn't.
  • It's less a "how to win" manual than an exposé of market structure — more useful for understanding why beating the books is hard than for extracting a specific edge.
Worth it if

you want a realistic, sobering picture of the sports betting ecosystem before deciding how — or whether — to compete in it.
