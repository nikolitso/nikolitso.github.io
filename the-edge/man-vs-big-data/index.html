Man vs Big Data

Where human football judgment still beats the algorithm — and where it doesn't.

by Stewart Cowley · 2017

Man vs Big Data looks at how data and algorithms are reshaping decisions once made on pure human judgment — from sports and dating to hiring and criminal justice — and where that shift genuinely improves outcomes versus where it introduces new, less visible biases.

The strongest material is in the case studies where algorithmic decision-making produced worse or more biased outcomes than the human judgment it replaced, a useful counterweight to the more triumphalist 'data beats intuition' narrative common in this genre.

It's a survey across several domains rather than a deep dive into one, which gives it breadth at some cost to depth in any single area.

The book's most useful service is refusing to let 'algorithmic' and 'objective' collapse into the same thing — Cowley's case studies show algorithms trained on biased historical data reproducing and sometimes amplifying exactly the human biases they were supposed to replace, which is a more specific and more useful critique than a general suspicion of data-driven decision-making.

Key ideas

  • Data and algorithms are reshaping decisions once made on pure human judgment — from sports and dating to hiring and criminal justice.
  • An algorithm trained on biased history doesn't remove the bias — it just makes it harder to see.
  • The strongest material shows algorithmic decision-making producing worse or more biased outcomes than the human judgment it replaced.
  • It's a survey across several domains rather than a deep dive into one, trading depth in any single area for breadth.
Worth it if

you want a balanced look at where algorithmic decision-making helps and where it quietly introduces new problems.
