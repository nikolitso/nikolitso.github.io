Empathy

Making the case for empathy as a practiced skill, not just a feeling.

by Roman Krznaric · 2014

Share on X

Roman Krznaric makes a bigger claim than most books that use the word “empathy” in the title: that it's a trainable skill with real social and political consequences, not just a nice personality trait some people happen to have more of.

The “handbook for revolution” framing is deliberate — Krznaric traces how empathy has historically driven social change, from abolition movements to civil rights, and argues that deliberately building the skill (through concrete practices, not just good intentions) has stakes well beyond your own relationships.

Krznaric distinguishes empathy from sympathy carefully — sympathy feels for someone from the outside, while the empathy he's arguing for requires actually stepping into another person's frame of reference, and he treats that as a discipline you build through specific practice (deliberate conversations across difference, structured perspective-taking exercises) rather than something you either have or don't.

Key ideas

  • Empathy is a trainable skill with real social and political consequences, not just a nice personality trait some people happen to have more of.
  • Empathy isn't a fixed trait some people have more of — it's a skill you build with deliberate practice, the same way you'd build any other.
  • Empathy has historically driven social change, from abolition movements to civil rights — the stakes go well beyond your own relationships.
  • Sympathy feels for someone from the outside; the empathy Krznaric argues for requires actually stepping into another person's frame of reference.
Worth it if

you want empathy treated as a skill to actively build rather than a trait you either have or don’t, with genuine intellectual weight behind the argument.
