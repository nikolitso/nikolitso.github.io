Beyond the Odds

A professional handicapper's statistical process for finding real edges.

by Elihu D. Feustel · 2024

Share on X

Feustel is a professional handicapper best known for horse racing, and this book distills his statistical approach to finding genuine value against the market — the core argument being that most bettors lose not because they can't predict outcomes, but because they don't understand the gap between prediction and pricing.

It's dense with real methodology: building power ratings, adjusting for track, surface, and pace factors, and — critically — sizing bets once an edge actually exists rather than betting on conviction alone.

It's not a beginner's book, and it leans heavily on horse racing examples that need translating if your interest sits elsewhere in sports betting; the prose is functional rather than engaging, and some technical passages reward re-reading more than speed-reading.

The book's real contribution is insisting that finding an edge and sizing a bet are two separate skills that most bettors conflate — Feustel treats conviction and bet size as almost unrelated questions, with sizing governed by the size of the edge and bankroll math rather than how confident a handicapper feels about a given pick.

Key ideas

  • Most bettors lose not because they can't predict outcomes, but because they don't understand the gap between prediction and pricing.
  • How sure you are about a bet and how much you should bet on it are two different calculations.
  • The methodology is dense and real: power ratings, adjustments for track and pace, and disciplined bet sizing once an edge actually exists.
  • It's not a beginner's book, and its horse-racing examples need translating if your interest sits elsewhere in sports betting.
Worth it if

you already have a working sense of what "edge" means and want to see a professional's actual process for turning a statistical model into disciplined, profitable action.
