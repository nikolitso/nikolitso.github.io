Squares & Sharps, Suckers & Sharks

Inside the culture war between casual bettors and professional sharps.

by Joseph Buchdahl · 2016

Share on X

Buchdahl's focus here isn't betting mechanics at all — it's psychology: why a small group of bettors (“sharps”) consistently win over time while the much larger group (“squares”) consistently don't, even when both have access to the same odds.

He explicitly interrogates whether betting markets can be reliably beaten at all, and whether the tipster-service industry built around “expert picks” actually has any real value — a genuinely skeptical, myth-puncturing angle for a book in this genre.

Traditionally published through Oldcastle Books rather than self-published, and Buchdahl's ongoing work running Football-Data.co.uk gives him real standing in the space — one of the more credentialed entries in this whole batch.

Buchdahl's skepticism toward the tipster industry is the book's sharpest material — he walks through why a service can post a genuinely impressive win rate over a cherry-picked sample while still being worthless or worse for a subscriber following it in real time, a distinction most marketing around paid tips is specifically designed to obscure.

Key ideas

  • A small group of bettors ("sharps") consistently win over time while a much larger group ("squares") consistently don't, even with access to the same odds.
  • A tipster's past win rate tells you almost nothing about whether following them going forward is a good bet.
