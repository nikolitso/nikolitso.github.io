Unsubscribe

Escaping the always-on email anxiety that eats real focused work.

by Jocelyn K. Glei · 2016

Share on X

Jocelyn K. Glei (formerly of Behance's 99U) narrows in on one specific productivity killer rather than tackling attention and focus broadly: email, and the particular anxiety of an always-refilling inbox that feels urgent even when it mostly isn't.

That narrower scope is actually the strength — general productivity advice tends to be vague enough to nod along with and then not apply; a book focused specifically on inbox habits gives you something concrete to change this week, not a philosophy to slowly absorb.

Glei is specific about the mechanism, not just the symptom — an inbox creates a false sense of urgency because every message arrives with equal visual weight regardless of actual importance, which is a design problem as much as a discipline problem, and her fixes (batching, triage rules, protected focus windows) target that structural flaw rather than just asking readers to feel less anxious about it.

Key ideas

  • Glei narrows in on one specific productivity killer rather than tackling attention and focus broadly: email, and the anxiety of an always-refilling inbox.
  • Email anxiety isn't a willpower problem — it's what happens when a tool gives every message the same visual urgency.
  • General productivity advice tends to be vague enough to nod along with and then not apply; a narrower scope gives something concrete to change this week.
  • Batching, triage rules, and protected focus windows target the structural flaw directly rather than just asking readers to feel less anxious.
Worth it if

email specifically (not focus in general) is the thing eating your day, and you want targeted tactics rather than a broader productivity framework.
