The Bookie

Decades running a Vegas sportsbook, told from the side of the house.

by Art Manteris · 2026

Share on X

Manteris ran sportsbooks in Las Vegas for decades, and this book is his insider account of the job — how odds actually get set and moved, how bookmakers manage risk against sharp bettors and public money alike, and the pressure of running a book during major events.

It's memoir as much as instruction, told from the side of the business that most betting books never show directly.

Because it's written from decades of specific Vegas experience, some of the operational detail is dated relative to today's largely online, algorithm-driven sportsbook industry, so treat it as a window into how the trade traditionally worked rather than a current operations manual.

Manteris is at his best describing the adversarial relationship between the book and its sharpest customers — the constant, largely invisible negotiation over where a line should sit, and how quickly a book has to react when professional money starts moving a number. It's a useful corrective for anyone who thinks of odds as a fixed statement of probability rather than a live, defensive instrument.

Key ideas

  • An odds line isn't a prediction — it's a moving target the book is constantly defending.
  • Bookmakers manage risk against both sharp bettors and public money simultaneously, not just set a number and wait.
  • The constant, largely invisible negotiation over where a line should sit is the real skill of running a book.
  • Because it's grounded in decades of Vegas-specific experience, treat it as a window into how the trade traditionally worked rather than a current operations manual.
Worth it if

you want to understand betting from the house's side of the counter — useful context for any bettor trying to understand why lines move the way they do.
