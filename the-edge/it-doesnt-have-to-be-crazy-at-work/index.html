It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work

The case against grind culture, from founders who built a calmer company.

by DHH, Jason Fried · 2018

Share on X

The Basecamp founders make the case against the grind culture and constant availability that many companies treat as inevitable, arguing instead for shorter workweeks, more focus time, and calm as a deliberate competitive advantage rather than a luxury.

It builds on their earlier philosophy, but sharpens the focus specifically on reducing anxiety and busyness inside a company.

It's written from the specific vantage point of a small, profitable, remote-friendly software company, so some prescriptions translate more easily to similar organizations than to larger or less flexible ones — treat it as a philosophy and a set of provocations rather than a universal operating manual.

Fried and Hansson are specific about what they cut to make calm possible — no open-ended chat culture, no expectation of after-hours responsiveness, meetings treated as a last resort rather than a default — which makes the book less a philosophy of 'work less' and more a detailed account of the structural choices that make lower intensity sustainable rather than just aspirational.

Key ideas

  • The Basecamp founders make the case for shorter workweeks, more focus time, and calm as a deliberate competitive advantage rather than a luxury.
  • Calm at work isn't a mindset — it's the result of specific things a company chooses not to do.
  • No open-ended chat culture, no expectation of after-hours responsiveness, meetings treated as a last resort rather than a default.
  • It's written from the vantage point of a small, profitable, remote-friendly company, so some prescriptions translate more easily to similar organizations than to larger ones.
Worth it if

you're a founder, manager, or team lead frustrated with always-on culture and want a concrete alternative philosophy.
