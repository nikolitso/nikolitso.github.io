The Anthology of Balaji

A career's worth of essays, tweets, and interviews on technology, truth, and building things — curated by Eric Jorgenson.

by Eric Jorgenson, Balaji Srinivasan · 2023

Share on X

Jorgenson compiles Balaji Srinivasan's essays, tweets, and podcast appearances into a single career-spanning collection built around three pillars: how technology is reshaping the world, how to think clearly under a flood of bad information, and how to actually go build something instead of just commentating.

It follows a similar format to Jorgenson's other author-anthology projects — extracted, arranged, and lightly annotated source material rather than a single connected argument — pulled from over a decade of Srinivasan's writing on startups, media, decentralization, and institutional trust.

The "Truth" section is the most conceptually ambitious of the three — rather than treating truth as singular, Srinivasan distinguishes between scientific, technical, political, economic, and cryptographic truth, arguing that blockchain's verifiable consensus is a genuinely new category that doesn't depend on trusting an intermediary the way the others do. The "Building" section is where the anthology is most quotable, organized around a recurring imperative — "Don't argue. Build." — that treats a working product as a more persuasive argument than any amount of debate.

The fragmented, quote-anthology format means depth varies wildly page to page, and Srinivasan's more polarizing political and institutional claims arrive here without much pushback or context, since the book is curated by an admirer rather than a critic.

The three-pillar structure holds up better than the fragmented format suggests, mostly because Jorgenson's editing keeps returning to a single throughline — that institutional trust is collapsing faster than most people have priced in, and the response should be building parallel systems rather than trying to reform the old ones from within. Whether that diagnosis is right is a separate question from whether it's argued well, and the anthology format makes it hard to press on the weaker claims.

Key ideas

  • Built around three pillars — Technology, Truth, and Building the Future — compiled from over a decade of Srinivasan's tweets, talks, and essays.
  • Truth isn't singular: Srinivasan separates scientific, technical, political, economic, and cryptographic truth, treating blockchain's verifiable consensus as a genuinely new kind of truth that doesn't depend on trusting an intermediary.
  • "Don't argue. Build." is the anthology's recurring imperative — a working product is treated as a more persuasive argument than any amount of debate.
  • Institutional trust is collapsing faster than most people have priced in, and the proposed response is building parallel systems rather than reforming the old ones.

Notable quote: “Don’t argue. Build.” — Balaji Srinivasan

Worth it if

you want a fast, index-able tour through one of tech's more prolific thinkers, and you're comfortable evaluating the more contrarian claims yourself.
