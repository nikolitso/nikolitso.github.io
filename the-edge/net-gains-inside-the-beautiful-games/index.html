The Edge/Football

Net Gains

The people and ideas that pulled football's data revolution into the mainstream.

by Ryan O'Hanlon · 2022

Share on X

O'Hanlon, a football journalist, traces how data analytics moved from a fringe curiosity to a central part of how top clubs recruit, coach, and play, told through the people and ideas that drove the shift rather than through the math itself.

It's structured as narrative journalism — profiles of analysts, executives, and the clubs that bet early on data — rather than a technical text.

Readers hoping for a hands-on introduction to building models themselves will find this book tells the story around the numbers rather than teaching the numbers themselves.

O'Hanlon is good at showing how the same data-driven idea landed very differently depending on the club culture it entered — a model that got embraced at one club as a genuine edge got quietly shelved at another because the coaching staff never bought in, which is really a story about organizational change as much as football analytics specifically.

Key ideas

  • Data analytics moved from a fringe curiosity to a central part of how top clubs recruit, coach, and play.
  • The same data can be a competitive edge at one club and a filed-away report at another, depending entirely on whether anyone with power believed in it.
  • It's structured as narrative journalism — profiles of analysts, executives, and early-adopting clubs — rather than a technical text.
  • Readers hoping for a hands-on modeling introduction should look to Soccermatics or Soccer Analytics instead; this tells the story around the numbers.
Worth it if

you want the human and institutional story behind football's data revolution, especially alongside a more technical book for the mechanics.
