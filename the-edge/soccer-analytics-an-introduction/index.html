Soccer Analytics

A hands-on, code-first introduction to football analytics in R.

by Clive Beggs · 2024

Share on X

Most of the books on this shelf are written for readers who want to understand football analytics. This one is written for readers who want to do it. Part of the Chapman & Hall/CRC statistics series, it walks through building football models in R — actual code, actual data, actual output — rather than explaining metrics from the outside.

That makes it a different kind of read than most popular explainers of expected goals. This is closer to a textbook: expect data structures, statistical tests, and model-building exercises rather than narrative chapters. It's the natural next step once you've read enough popular football-analytics writing to want to check the work yourself instead of taking someone else's model on faith.

The exercises are the point — working through the R code yourself is what separates understanding a metric's definition from being able to reproduce and stress-test it, and the book is structured so each chapter's model builds on assumptions you've already coded and checked rather than taken on faith from the previous chapter.

Key ideas

  • Most books on this shelf are written for readers who want to understand football analytics — this one is written for readers who want to do it.
  • You don't really understand a football model until you've had to debug your own broken version of it.
  • It walks through building football models in R — actual code, actual data, actual output — rather than explaining metrics from the outside.
  • It's the natural next step once you've read enough popular football-analytics writing to want to check the work yourself.
Worth it if

you already know some R (or are willing to learn alongside the book) and want to move from reading about analytics to building it.
