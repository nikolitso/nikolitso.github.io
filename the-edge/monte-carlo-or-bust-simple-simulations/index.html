Monte Carlo Or Bust

Simple simulations for thinking probabilistically about sports bets.

by Joseph Buchdahl · 2021

Buchdahl is one of the more genuinely credentialed writers in sports betting — he also runs the analytics site Football-Data.co.uk — and this book tackles a specific technical tool: Monte Carlo simulation, for turning randomness into something you can actually model rather than just accept.

This book was reviewed favorably by Pinnacle's own educational resources as a detailed, practically-oriented guide to making randomness "work for you, rather than against you" — a notable endorsement given Pinnacle is a sportsbook, not exactly incentivized to help bettors win.

It's more technical than most betting books on this shelf, closer to applied statistics than gambling memoir, so it rewards readers comfortable getting hands-on with simulation rather than looking for a quick strategic edge.

Buchdahl's real service is making Monte Carlo simulation something you can actually build rather than just gesture at — the book walks through constructing simulations from scratch instead of treating them as a black box, which matters because the value of the technique is in being able to stress-test your own assumptions, not just trust someone else's output.

Key ideas

  • Monte Carlo simulation turns randomness into something you can actually model rather than just accept.
  • Simulation doesn't remove uncertainty — it just lets you see the whole range of outcomes instead of betting on the average one.
  • Buchdahl's real service is making the technique something you can build yourself rather than treat as a black box.
  • Pinnacle's own educational resources endorsed it favorably — notable given a sportsbook has no incentive to help bettors win.
Worth it if

you want a genuinely credentialed author's technical take on modeling uncertainty, not another gambling personality's war stories.
