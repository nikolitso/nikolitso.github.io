Manage Your Day-to-Day

Build Your Routine, Find Your Focus, and Sharpen Your Creative Mind

by Jocelyn K. Glei · 2013

Assembled from the 99U conference and its network of writers and researchers, this is a collection of short essays on protecting focus and building a sustainable creative routine — treating attention as the scarce resource that actually determines output, not raw hours worked.

Contributors including Gretchen Rubin, Tony Schwartz, and Scott Belsky each tackle a narrow piece of the problem — mornings, meetings, procrastination, rest — so the book reads more like a curated anthology of practical essays than a single unified system.

Because it's a multi-author anthology, quality and specificity vary chapter to chapter, and a few pieces lean on productivity-book platitudes that were already common by the time this was published; the value is in a handful of genuinely sharp essays rather than the whole being equally strong.

The strongest individual essays reframe attention as something actively spent rather than passively available — treating a calendar as a budget for focus rather than just a schedule of obligations, which is a small shift in framing that changes how readers evaluate whether a given meeting or task is actually worth the attention it costs.

Key ideas

  • Attention, not raw hours worked, is the scarce resource that actually determines output.
  • Your calendar isn't a record of your time — it's a budget for your attention, and most people never actually balance it.
  • Contributors including Gretchen Rubin, Tony Schwartz, and Scott Belsky each tackle a narrow piece of the problem rather than one unified system.
  • Because it's a multi-author anthology, quality and specificity vary chapter to chapter — the value is in a handful of genuinely sharp essays.
Worth it if

you want bite-sized, essay-length takes on focus and routine from a range of creative practitioners, rather than one author's single framework.
