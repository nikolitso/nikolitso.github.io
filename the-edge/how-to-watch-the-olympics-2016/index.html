How to Watch the Olympics

The rules and context that make Olympic sports worth watching closely.

by David Goldblatt, Johnny Acton · 2012

Share on X

How to Watch the Olympics is a sport-by-sport viewer's guide, published ahead of the Rio 2016 Games, explaining the rules, scoring quirks, and things to actually watch for in the roughly 40 Olympic sports most casual viewers only tune into once every four years.

The most useful sections are for the sports with genuinely confusing scoring — dressage, fencing, judo — where a few pages of explanation turns baffling TV coverage into something you can actually follow and enjoy.

It's necessarily a reference book tied to a specific Games cycle rather than a timeless read — useful in the run-up to an Olympics, less so afterward except as a general primer on the sports themselves.

The dressage and fencing chapters do the most work for a casual viewer specifically because those sports' scoring is invisible without context — a fencing touch can happen too fast to see live, and dressage scoring rewards subtleties (rhythm, straightness) that look identical to an untrained eye, so a few pages of explanation genuinely changes what you're able to notice during the broadcast rather than just adding trivia.

Key ideas

  • A sport-by-sport viewer's guide explains the rules and scoring quirks of the roughly 40 Olympic sports most casual viewers only tune into every four years.
  • The sports that benefit most from a viewer's guide are the ones where the scoring itself is the part you can't see without help.
  • Dressage, fencing, and judo get the most useful treatment — a fencing touch can happen too fast to see live, and dressage rewards subtleties an untrained eye misses.
  • It's necessarily tied to a specific Games cycle rather than a timeless read, useful in the run-up to an Olympics more than afterward.
Worth it if

you want to actually understand what's happening in the Olympic sports you don't follow the rest of the four-year cycle.
