Changing the Game

Turning a passion for sports into an actual profitable business.

by William O. Hall III · 2012

Hall pitches this as Wall Street investment principles applied to sports betting and fantasy sports, built around his own “Get In and Win” system, with Billy Beane and Mark Cuban invoked as the data-driven-sports-management touchstones.

The concrete, usable piece is the risk management: capping any single wager at around 5% of your bankroll, treating betting explicitly as “a legitimate game of skill” rather than pure chance, with structure borrowed from how position sizing works in real investing.

Worth flagging plainly: the “Wall Street Investment Manager” credential comes from the book's own subtitle and marketing rather than something independently verifiable in what's publicly documented — treat the investing-pedigree framing as the author's own positioning.

The 5%-of-bankroll cap is the one piece of the book worth taking regardless of how you feel about the broader Wall Street framing — it's a concrete, checkable rule that prevents the single most common way undisciplined bettors go broke: sizing a bet to match conviction rather than to survive being wrong, which any position-sizing framework borrowed from real risk management is built specifically to prevent.

Key ideas

  • Hall pitches Wall Street investment principles applied to sports betting and fantasy sports, with Billy Beane and Mark Cuban as data-driven touchstones.
  • The size of your bet should be governed by your bankroll math, not by how confident you feel about the pick.
  • Capping any single wager at around 5% of bankroll prevents the most common way undisciplined bettors go broke.
  • The "Wall Street Investment Manager" credential comes from the book's own marketing rather than something independently verifiable — treat that framing with appropriate caution.
Worth it if

you like the investing-analogy approach to betting and don't need the author's credentials independently verified to get value from the framework.
