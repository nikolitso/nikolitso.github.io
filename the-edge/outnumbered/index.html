Outnumbered

How algorithms are reshaping decisions once left to human judgment.

by David Sumpter · 2018

David Sumpter, a mathematician, wrote Outnumbered to demystify the algorithms actually running Facebook's feed, Google's search results, dating apps, and the fake-news and filter-bubble dynamics they produce — explaining the real math behind headlines about AI, rather than either hyping or dismissing it.

The most valuable material is where Sumpter shows the algorithms are often simpler and less mysterious than public discourse assumes — a useful corrective for anyone whose understanding of 'the algorithm' comes mostly from vague, alarmist framing.

As a mathematician writing for a general audience, Sumpter is careful to separate what the math genuinely supports from where public fear has outrun the actual technical capability.

Sumpter's chapter on dating apps is the clearest demonstration of his broader point — the matching algorithms are simpler and more mechanical than the mystique around them suggests, and understanding the actual math (largely variations on collaborative filtering) demystifies a system most users experience as an inscrutable black box shaping outcomes that feel deeply personal.

Key ideas

  • Sumpter demystifies the algorithms actually running Facebook's feed, Google's search, dating apps, and filter-bubble dynamics.
  • The algorithms shaping your feed and your matches are usually simpler than the mystery built up around them.
  • The dating-app chapter shows matching algorithms are largely variations on collaborative filtering, not an inscrutable black box.
  • As a mathematician writing for a general audience, Sumpter separates what the math genuinely supports from where public fear has outrun the technology.
Worth it if

you want a technically grounded explanation of how social media and search algorithms actually work, without the hype.
