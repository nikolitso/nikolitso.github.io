How to Get on With Your Colleagues

Practical fixes for the everyday friction of working with other people.

by The School of Life · 2020

Most workplace-relationship advice is either HR-department-generic (“communicate openly!”) or purely tactical (how to run a meeting). This one stays closer to the imprint’s actual strength: the psychological undercurrents that make some colleague relationships easy and others quietly draining, and why “just communicate better” is true but not very actionable on its own.

The School of Life's real contribution is naming the specific psychological patterns that make a colleague relationship curdle — projecting a difficult parent onto a difficult boss, reading neutral feedback as a personal verdict — patterns that generic workplace-communication advice never touches because it assumes rational actors rather than people carrying their whole emotional history into a Monday standup.

Key ideas

  • "Just communicate better" is true but not very actionable on its own — the real issue is usually the psychological undercurrent beneath a strained relationship.
  • Most difficult colleagues aren't a communication problem — they're an old pattern wearing a new face.
  • A colleague's overreaction to feedback often has nothing to do with the current situation and everything to do with an old emotional script.
  • It narrows in specifically on peer-to-peer relationships, which make or break how bearable a job actually is day to day.
Worth it if

a specific colleague relationship has been harder than it should be, and you want language for what’s actually going on rather than generic collaboration tips.
