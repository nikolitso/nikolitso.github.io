Maximize Your Potential

Grow Your Expertise, Take Bold Risks & Build an Incredible Career

by Jocelyn K. Glei · 2013

Share on X

The second book in the 99U series turns from daily habits to career trajectory: how to develop genuine expertise, when to take a risk on an opportunity that isn't fully ready, and how to build a body of work that compounds instead of a series of disconnected gigs.

Like its predecessor, it's a curated set of essays from working creatives and entrepreneurs, including Elizabeth Gilbert and Seth Godin, organized loosely around expertise, opportunity, and momentum rather than a single author's argument.

The anthology format again means uneven depth — some essays are closer to conference-talk transcripts than developed arguments — and the advice occasionally reflects the specific creative-industry, conference-circuit world it was drawn from more than a general career context.

The essays on taking underprepared opportunities are the collection's sharpest thread — several contributors make the case, backed by their own career turns, that waiting to feel fully ready before saying yes to a stretch opportunity usually means waiting until someone less qualified but more willing has already taken it.

Key ideas

  • The second 99U book turns from daily habits to career trajectory — developing genuine expertise and building a body of work that compounds.
  • The opportunity that feels like you're not ready yet is usually the one worth taking before someone else does.
  • Waiting to feel fully ready before saying yes to a stretch opportunity usually means someone less qualified but more willing takes it first.
  • The anthology format means uneven depth — some essays read closer to conference-talk transcripts than developed arguments.
Worth it if

you're early-to-mid career in a creative field and want varied, essay-length perspectives on building expertise rather than a single prescriptive career framework.
