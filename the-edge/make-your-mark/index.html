Make Your Mark

The Creative's Guide to Building a Business with Impact

by Jocelyn K. Glei · 2014

Share on X

The third and final 99U book shifts from individual craft to actually building something — turning creative skill into a business with real income and a team, aimed at makers who are strong on the creative side but less sure how to build a sustainable venture around it.

Again structured as curated essays from entrepreneurs and creative directors, it covers scoping a viable business idea, pricing and positioning creative work, and scaling past a one-person operation without losing what made the work distinctive.

The essay format keeps individual pieces short and accessible, but as with the earlier two books, that also means no single idea gets developed into a full, tested framework — it works better as a series of prompts and starting points than a business plan.

The most consistently useful essays are on pricing — several contributors push back directly on the instinct to underprice creative work out of fear of rejection, making the case that pricing too low doesn't just cost income, it actively signals lower quality to the exact clients a creative business needs to attract.

Key ideas

  • The third 99U book shifts from individual craft to actually building something — turning creative skill into a business with real income.
  • Underpricing your work doesn't just cost you money — it tells the market you don't believe in it either.
  • Pricing too low doesn't just cost income, it actively signals lower quality to the exact clients a creative business needs to attract.
  • The essay format keeps individual pieces short and accessible, but no single idea gets developed into a full, tested framework.
Worth it if

you're a creative professional weighing whether and how to build a business around your work, and want varied first-hand perspectives rather than one consultant's playbook.
