Becoming a Winning Gambler

What separates bettors who survive long-term from those who don't.

by Allen Moody · 2013

Share on X

Moody's broader gambling book, expanding past sports into blackjack (card counting and basic strategy), poker, and horse racing, with separate sports-betting chapters split out by league — baseball, NBA, NFL, college football, college basketball.

On the money-management side, it walks through established systems like the Martingale and the Kelly Criterion alongside what's described as an original framework of Moody's own, giving readers a comparative view of bankroll approaches rather than pushing just one.

Because it's a general gambling book rather than a sports-specific one, the sports-betting content is only part of the whole — readers who want betting content exclusively may find a more dedicated title a tighter fit.

Laying the Martingale and Kelly Criterion side by side is more instructive than either system alone — Moody uses the comparison to show why Martingale's appeal (chase a loss, recover it with one win) is really a bankroll-ruin strategy dressed up as a system, while Kelly's more conservative, edge-proportional sizing is the one actually built to survive a losing streak.

Key ideas

  • The book expands past sports into blackjack, poker, and horse racing, with sports chapters split out by league.
  • A betting system that promises to recover every loss with the next bet is usually a fast way to lose your whole bankroll, not a safety net.
  • The Martingale's appeal — chase a loss, recover it with one win — is really a bankroll-ruin strategy dressed up as a system.
  • Kelly's more conservative, edge-proportional sizing is the one actually built to survive a losing streak.
Worth it if

you want one book covering multiple forms of gambling rather than sports betting in isolation.
