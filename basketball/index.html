Workings, match by match

Standings

  • #: rank by Power (not by wins). The lines mark the play-off places and relegation.
  • W–L: wins and losses.
  • Power: how good a team really is, in points per 100 possessions better (+) or worse (−) than an average team on a neutral court. Each game's margin is corrected for the opponent's strength and for home court (worth 3 points per 100 possessions). Early in the season it also leans on a starting rating (from pre-season odds and last season), worth 4 games, so one big win or loss moves it less than Net. The best guide to future results.
  • Net: the raw version: (points scored − points allowed) ÷ possessions × 100. No correction for opponents or home court.
  • Exp. W: the wins a team "deserves" from its points scored and allowed (Pythagorean expectation: games × PF14 ÷ (PF14 + PA14)).
  • Luck: actual wins − Exp. W. Above 0, the team won more than its scoring says (usually close games); this tends not to last.
  • Pace: possessions per 40 minutes, the speed a team plays at (higher = faster). Same as Basketball-Reference's Pace Factor, on 40 minutes instead of the NBA's 48: possessions × 40 ÷ minutes played, so overtime does not inflate it. Possessions per game = shots from the field − offensive rebounds + turnovers + 0.44 × free throws, averaged for the two teams. League average: –.
  • Played: average Power of the opponents already faced. 0 = an average schedule; above 0 = a tougher one, so the record so far understates the team.
  • Ahead: the same for the opponents still to play.

League stats

  • Net: points per 100 possessions better (or worse) than opponents, the best single predictor of future results. Adj Net: the same, corrected for the opponents faced and home court.
  • ORtg / DRtg: points scored / allowed per 100 possessions (lower DRtg = better defence). Offence is more repeatable than defence early on.
  • eFG%: shooting efficiency (a three counts 1.5×), the most important of the four factors, but it rides on 3-point shooting, which takes many games to settle. TOV%: turnovers per 100 possessions (lower = better), settles quickly. OREB% / DREB%: share of own misses and of opponents' misses rebounded; real skills that settle fast.

Vs Market

The bookmakers' balanced lines for every played game (where both sides are priced most evenly) against what happened. Click a column to sort.

Spread

Vs line = real margin − the margin the handicap expected: above 0, the team did better than the market priced it (underrated); below 0, worse (overrated).

Total

Vs line = points scored in the game − the total line, averaged over the team's games: positive = over, negative = under. O–U = overs and unders.

Every game

Team strength

  • All numbers are points per game against an average team on a neutral court (ratings are worked out per possession and turned into points with the league's usual number of possessions a game).
  • Available: the team's rating with the players expected for the next game: those who played its last game, unless the news says otherwise. +6.0 = six points a game better than an average team. This is the number the projections will use: home rating − away rating + home court ≈ the projected handicap.
  • Full strength: the same with every rotation player available.
  • Missed last game: rotation players (10+ minutes a game) who did not play the team's last game, so are expected out again, and how much weaker the team is without each: −1.7 = 1.7 points a game. Most are rested, not injured; confirmed out marks absences confirmed by the news; out all season = confirmed absent and not played yet, so there is no data to value him (the rating already reflects playing without him). Players the team rates no worse without (their minutes go to players rated higher) are not listed, but still count in Available.
  • Market: the rating the bookmakers' balanced handicaps imply, with the absences they priced added back. Early in the season it carries most of the weight (6 ÷ (6 + games played)), because three games say little.
  • Results: scores only: points a game better than opponents, corrected for the opponents faced and home court.
  • Players (pick a team below): each player's Box, On/off and Value, in points per game in his usual minutes, with the columns explained under the team's table.

    Box scores

      Team ratings

      Points scored and conceded per game this season, and the attack and defence ratings the model uses (1.00 = league average; for defence, lower is better). Click a column to sort.

      How it is builtOnly this season's played matches are used. 1. The league average points for home and away sides. 2. Each team's attack and defence against that average (1.00 = average, 1.10 = 10% more). 3. For each match: league average × the home team's attack × the away team's defence, and the same the other way round. 4. The difference of the two numbers, plus a small correction, is the projected margin.
      Winner and handicapThe team with the higher projected score is the projected winner. Its win chance comes from how far past margins in the league missed the projection. The edge on a handicap is the projected margin minus the book line: if the model has the home team winning by 9 and the book line is +3.5 (home favoured by 3.5), the home team covers by 5.5.
      Early in the seasonMatches are projected once both teams have played 3 games, but early numbers are rough: treat them with care. A handicap is flagged only once both teams have played 6 games and the edge is 5 points or more.
