<p>David Sumpter, a mathematician, wrote Outnumbered to demystify the algorithms actually running Facebook's feed, Google's search results, dating apps, and the fake-news and filter-bubble dynamics they produce — explaining the real math behind headlines about AI, rather than either hyping or dismissing it.</p>

<p>The most valuable material is where Sumpter shows the algorithms are often simpler and less mysterious than public discourse assumes — a useful corrective for anyone whose understanding of 'the algorithm' comes mostly from vague, alarmist framing.</p>

<p>As a mathematician writing for a general audience, Sumpter is careful to separate what the math genuinely supports from where public fear has outrun the actual technical capability.</p>

<p>Sumpter's chapter on dating apps is the clearest demonstration of his broader point — the matching algorithms are simpler and more mechanical than the mystique around them suggests, and understanding the actual math (largely variations on collaborative filtering) demystifies a system most users experience as an inscrutable black box shaping outcomes that feel deeply personal.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>Sumpter demystifies the algorithms actually running Facebook's feed, Google's search, dating apps, and filter-bubble dynamics.</li><li>The algorithms shaping your feed and your matches are usually simpler than the mystery built up around them.</li><li>The dating-app chapter shows matching algorithms are largely variations on collaborative filtering, not an inscrutable black box.</li><li>As a mathematician writing for a general audience, Sumpter separates what the math genuinely supports from where public fear has outrun the technology.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you want a technically grounded explanation of how social media and search algorithms actually work, without the hype.</p>
