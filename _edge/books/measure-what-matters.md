<p>Doerr, a venture capitalist who introduced Google to the OKR (Objectives and Key Results) framework early in its history, lays out the goal-setting system credited with shaping the discipline of some of the most successful companies in tech.</p>

<p>The book mixes Doerr's own account of bringing OKRs to Google with case studies from other organizations — Intel, where OKRs originated, the Gates Foundation, and others — showing the framework applied at different scales.</p>

<p>It's as much a business-history and case-study collection as an instructional manual, so readers wanting a pure how-to guide may need to extract the practical steps from the surrounding narrative; the core framework itself is simple enough to summarize in a paragraph.</p>

<p>Doerr is careful to separate OKRs from performance reviews — a distinction a lot of companies that adopt the framework blur, to its detriment. His argument is that OKRs work as an aspirational, transparent goal-tracking tool precisely because they aren't tied to compensation or evaluation; the moment they become a scorecard for individual performance, people stop setting ambitious targets and start sandbagging.</p>
