<p>Jocelyn K. Glei (formerly of Behance's 99U) narrows in on one specific productivity killer rather than tackling attention and focus broadly: email, and the particular anxiety of an always-refilling inbox that feels urgent even when it mostly isn't.</p>

<p>That narrower scope is actually the strength — general productivity advice tends to be vague enough to nod along with and then not apply; a book focused specifically on inbox habits gives you something concrete to change this week, not a philosophy to slowly absorb.</p>

<p>Glei is specific about the mechanism, not just the symptom — an inbox creates a false sense of urgency because every message arrives with equal visual weight regardless of actual importance, which is a design problem as much as a discipline problem, and her fixes (batching, triage rules, protected focus windows) target that structural flaw rather than just asking readers to feel less anxious about it.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>Glei narrows in on one specific productivity killer rather than tackling attention and focus broadly: email, and the anxiety of an always-refilling inbox.</li><li>Email anxiety isn't a willpower problem — it's what happens when a tool gives every message the same visual urgency.</li><li>General productivity advice tends to be vague enough to nod along with and then not apply; a narrower scope gives something concrete to change this week.</li><li>Batching, triage rules, and protected focus windows target the structural flaw directly rather than just asking readers to feel less anxious.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> email specifically (not focus in general) is the thing eating your day, and you want targeted tactics rather than a broader productivity framework.</p>
