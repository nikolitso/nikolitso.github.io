<p>“Follow your passion” is famously bad career advice — vague, and it assumes you already know what your passion is. This entry pushes past that platitude into the harder, more useful question underneath it: what does “fulfilling” actually mean for you specifically, since it’s clearly not the same thing for everyone, and most people have never actually sat down and defined it before chasing it.</p>

<p>The School of Life's more useful move is separating 'fulfilling' into distinct, sometimes competing ingredients — status, mastery, autonomy, connection to a larger purpose — rather than treating it as one single feeling to chase, since a job can deliver heavily on one of these and barely register on another, and most career dissatisfaction traces to an unexamined mismatch between which of these a person actually needs and which their current job happens to supply.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>"Follow your passion" is famously bad advice — vague, and it assumes you already know what your passion is.</li><li>"Fulfilling work" isn't one feeling — it's several different ingredients, and most job dissatisfaction comes from getting the wrong mix.</li><li>Status, mastery, autonomy, and connection to a larger purpose can compete rather than align in a single job.</li><li>Defining what "fulfilling" actually means for you specifically has to come before chasing it.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> “fulfilling work” has been a goal you’ve chased without ever quite defining, and you want to do that definitional work before the next career decision instead of after.</p>
