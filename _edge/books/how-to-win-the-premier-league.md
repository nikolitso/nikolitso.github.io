<p>Ian Graham ran Liverpool’s analytics department through the Klopp era — the actual research director behind the recruitment model that helped turn a mid-table-adjacent club into repeat title contenders. How to Win the Premier League is his account of doing the job from the inside, which makes it a rarer artifact than most books on this shelf: not a journalist or an academic explaining analytics from outside a club, but the person who built the model explaining what it actually took to get a boardroom, a manager, and a scouting department to trust it.</p>

<p>That insider angle is the whole appeal. Most football-analytics writing has to guess at how clubs actually use data day to day; this one doesn’t have to guess.</p>

<p>The most valuable material isn't the modeling itself but Graham's account of the organizational fight to get a model trusted — convincing a manager to sign off on a recruitment target the eye test disagreed with, and building enough credibility inside the club that the data department's calls carried real weight in the transfer market. That politics-of-persuasion layer is what most outside accounts of football analytics can't access.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>A good model is worthless if nobody with the authority to act on it actually trusts it.</li><li>Ian Graham writes from inside the room — the actual research director behind Liverpool's recruitment model, not an outside journalist guessing at how clubs use data.</li><li>The real work wasn't the modeling itself but the organizational fight to get a recruitment target the eye test disagreed with actually signed off.</li><li>Most football-analytics writing has to guess at how clubs use data day to day; this account doesn't have to guess.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you want the closest thing to a first-hand account of analytics actually running recruitment at a title-winning club, rather than an outsider’s theory of how it should work.</p>
