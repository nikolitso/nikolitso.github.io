<p><strong>Kevin Pullein</strong> is a professional sports betting analyst and author, best known for his long-running work at Sporting Life and Racing Post, where he has written extensively on the mathematics of football betting markets.</p>

<p>Part of the Racing Post Expert Series, this book is a rigorous, data-driven guide to finding value in football betting markets. Pullein walks through how to build simple statistical models for predicting match outcomes, how bookmakers price odds and where their margins hide, and how to think in terms of probability and expected value rather than gut instinct or tipster hunches. It's less about "picks" and more about building a repeatable, disciplined process — estimating true probabilities, comparing them to the odds on offer, and only betting when the numbers say there's an edge.</p>

<p>A useful read for anyone who wants to apply an analytical, quant mindset to football markets rather than relying on narrative or recency bias.</p>

<p>Pullein is unusually disciplined about separating the modeling step from the betting step — building an honest probability estimate first, entirely uninfluenced by what the market is offering, and only afterward comparing that number to the odds to see if a genuine gap exists. That ordering matters more than it sounds: bettors who look at the odds first tend to unconsciously anchor their own estimate toward the market's number, which quietly erases the edge they were trying to find.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>Pullein builds simple statistical models for predicting match outcomes and shows how bookmakers price odds and where their margins hide.</li><li>Build your own probability estimate before you look at the odds — looking first quietly anchors your judgment to the market's number.</li><li>It's less about "picks" and more about building a repeatable, disciplined process: estimate true probabilities, compare them to the odds, bet only when there's an edge.</li><li>It applies an analytical, quant mindset to football markets rather than relying on narrative or recency bias.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you want to build an actual quantitative process for football betting — probability and expected value — rather than another book of tips or hot takes.</p>
