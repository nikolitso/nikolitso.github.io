<p>Julie Zhuo became a manager at Facebook at 25, with no formal training and no idea what she was doing beyond the job title — and The Making of a Manager is the honest, memoir-adjacent account of learning the role from inside it, rather than a top-down framework written by someone who forgot what it felt like to be new at this.</p>

<p>That’s exactly why it’s become the go-to first read for anyone handed a team for the first time: it doesn’t assume you already have management instincts, and it walks through the actual awkward moments (your first one-on-one, your first difficult feedback conversation, the first time a report is upset with you) with real specificity instead of abstract principles.</p>

<p>Zhuo is unusually candid about the moments most management books gloss over entirely — the report who quietly disengages instead of complaining, the meeting where you realize you've lost the room, the mistake you only recognize as a mistake months later. The book's structure follows her own learning curve rather than a tidy framework, which is part of why it reads more like a trustworthy colleague's notes than a consultant's model.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>Julie Zhuo became a manager at Facebook at 25 with no formal training, and this is the honest account of learning the role from inside it.</li><li>You don't learn to manage from a framework — you learn it from surviving your own worst one-on-ones.</li><li>It doesn't assume you already have management instincts — it walks through the actual awkward first moments with real specificity.</li><li>The structure follows Zhuo's own learning curve rather than a tidy framework, reading more like a trustworthy colleague's notes than a consultant's model.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you’re newly managing people for the first time and want something concrete and honest rather than a management-theory textbook.</p>
