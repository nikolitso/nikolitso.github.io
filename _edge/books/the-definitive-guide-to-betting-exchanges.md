<p>Edited by Paul Kealy, this Racing Post guide focuses specifically on exchange betting — how exchanges like Betfair actually function, how to place back and lay bets, and how trading strategies differ from traditional wagering.</p>

<p>As an edited volume it draws on multiple contributors' expertise to cover the exchange landscape more broadly than a single trader's memoir would.</p>

<p>Like most exchange guides, its platform and market details have dated somewhat since publication, so the lasting value is in the conceptual grounding — liquidity, price formation, the mechanics of laying a bet — rather than the specifics.</p>

<p>The most durable conceptual distinction in the book is between backing and laying — backing a bet is a standard wager on an outcome happening, while laying is effectively acting as the bookmaker on that same outcome, and understanding that an exchange lets any user do either is what actually explains why exchange odds behave differently from a traditional bookmaker's fixed price.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>This Racing Post guide focuses specifically on exchange betting — how exchanges like Betfair actually function and how back and lay bets work.</li><li>On an exchange you can bet like a customer or price like a bookmaker — most bettors never realize the second option exists.</li><li>Backing is a standard wager on an outcome happening; laying is effectively acting as the bookmaker on that same outcome.</li><li>As an edited volume, it draws on multiple contributors to cover the exchange landscape more broadly than a single trader's memoir would.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> exchange betting specifically, rather than sports betting broadly, is your focus and you want a structured primer on how it works.</p>
