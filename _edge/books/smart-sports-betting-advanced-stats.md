<p>Rudnitsky opens with his own cautionary story — turning a $100 gift into roughly $8,000 before losing all of it — and the book is his attempt to distill what he learned studying successful bettors afterward.</p>

<p>It's deliberately accessible rather than technical: bankroll management, understanding “fair odds” and value, avoiding the specific cognitive biases that wreck casual bettors, and a pointed critique of how sports media covers betting picks — endorsed by ESPN betting writer David Purdum, and reportedly a category bestseller on Amazon.</p>

<p>This is explicitly a beginner's book, not a sophisticated modeling guide — readers looking for more technical rigor should look elsewhere on this shelf instead.</p>

<p>Rudnitsky's own losing streak does real work as a structural device — he's specific about the exact cognitive traps that took him from up $8,000 to broke (chasing losses, upping stakes to recover faster, mistaking a hot streak for skill), which makes the caution more credible coming from someone narrating his own mistakes than from a hypothetical cautionary tale.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>
