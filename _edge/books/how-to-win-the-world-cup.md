<p>Evans interviews the managers and coaches behind winning World Cup campaigns, distilling their approaches to squad building, tactics, and — especially — the human and psychological side of leading a team through a short, high-pressure tournament rather than a long league season.</p>

<p>The format is largely oral history and profile rather than a single unified framework, letting different managers' philosophies sit side by side, sometimes in tension with each other.</p>

<p>Because tournament football is a small-sample, high-variance environment, some of the lessons read more like compelling narrative than generalizable strategy — worth keeping in mind before treating any one manager's approach as a formula.</p>

<p>The strongest material comes from the contrasts between managers rather than any single interview — the book is most interesting when a defensively-minded tournament winner and an attacking idealist describe the same pressure-cooker environment in almost opposite terms, and Evans lets those disagreements sit unresolved rather than forcing a unified theory of tournament success.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>A World Cup is won as much by managing six weeks of pressure as by any tactical system.</li><li>The most interesting material comes from contrasts between managers rather than any single interview, with different philosophies left to sit in tension.</li><li>Squad building and the psychological side of a short tournament matter as much as on-field tactics.</li><li>Because tournament football is small-sample and high-variance, some lessons read more as compelling narrative than generalizable strategy.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you're interested in football management and leadership specifically in short, high-stakes tournament settings, alongside the more data-driven books on this shelf.</p>
