<p>Moody spent years as About.com's “Sports Gambling Guide” columnist, and this book compiles and refines that material into a single beginner-oriented primer covering both team sports and horse racing.</p>

<p>The combined scope — mainstream sports betting alongside horse racing wagering — is what sets it apart from most single-sport guides on this shelf, giving newer bettors a broader map of how different betting markets actually work before specializing.</p>

<p>As a genuinely introductory title, don't expect the technical depth of books like Weighing the Odds — this is closer to an explainer for someone who's never placed a serious bet than a strategy manual for an experienced one.</p>

<p>The combined team-sports-and-horse-racing scope is more valuable than it first appears — Moody uses the structural similarities between the two (how a line moves, what public versus sharp money looks like, where the house edge actually sits) to teach transferable betting literacy rather than sport-specific tips that stop being useful the moment you bet on something else.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>Moody's years as About.com's "Sports Gambling Guide" columnist get compiled into a single beginner-oriented primer covering both team sports and horse racing.</li><li>The underlying mechanics of a betting market are more similar across sports than most beginners assume — learn those first, not the picks.</li><li>Learning how a line moves and what public versus sharp money looks like transfers across sports better than sport-specific tips do.</li><li>As a genuinely introductory title, don't expect the technical depth of a book like Weighing the Odds.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you want one broad, beginner-friendly primer covering multiple betting formats instead of a single-sport deep dive.</p>
