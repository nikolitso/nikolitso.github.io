<p>This is one of the more academically credentialed books on the shelf — a Princeton University Press title that treats sports math with the same rigor you'd expect from an actual statistics course, not a pop-science gloss.</p>

<p>It covers a genuinely wide range of real decision problems: baseball park effects and hitter valuation, the math behind NFL fourth-down and two-point-conversion decisions, basketball player evaluation (including a case study built around referee Tim Donaghy's betting scandal), and payroll-versus-performance relationships across leagues.</p>

<p>The spreadsheet-based tools throughout mean it rewards readers willing to actually work through the models, not just read the conclusions — closer to a textbook you'd keep on a shelf than a book you read once and shelve.</p>

<p>The Tim Donaghy case study is the book's sharpest material precisely because it's real stakes rather than a hypothetical — Winston uses actual betting-line data from games Donaghy officiated to show statistically detectable anomalies in outcomes relative to the spread, walking through exactly the kind of forensic analysis that turned suspicion into a provable case rather than just speculation.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>A Princeton University Press title that treats sports math with the rigor of an actual statistics course, not a pop-science gloss.</li><li>Betting-line data isn't just for finding an edge — it's precise enough to help catch a referee fixing games.</li><li>It covers baseball park effects, the math behind NFL fourth-down decisions, basketball player evaluation, and payroll-versus-performance relationships.</li><li>The spreadsheet-based tools throughout mean it rewards readers willing to actually work through the models, not just read the conclusions.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you want the rigorous, university-press version of sports math, with real tools to work through rather than just takeaways to skim.</p>
