<p>Moody's broader gambling book, expanding past sports into blackjack (card counting and basic strategy), poker, and horse racing, with separate sports-betting chapters split out by league — baseball, NBA, NFL, college football, college basketball.</p>

<p>On the money-management side, it walks through established systems like the Martingale and the Kelly Criterion alongside what's described as an original framework of Moody's own, giving readers a comparative view of bankroll approaches rather than pushing just one.</p>

<p>Because it's a general gambling book rather than a sports-specific one, the sports-betting content is only part of the whole — readers who want betting content exclusively may find a more dedicated title a tighter fit.</p>

<p>Laying the Martingale and Kelly Criterion side by side is more instructive than either system alone — Moody uses the comparison to show why Martingale's appeal (chase a loss, recover it with one win) is really a bankroll-ruin strategy dressed up as a system, while Kelly's more conservative, edge-proportional sizing is the one actually built to survive a losing streak.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>The book expands past sports into blackjack, poker, and horse racing, with sports chapters split out by league.</li><li>A betting system that promises to recover every loss with the next bet is usually a fast way to lose your whole bankroll, not a safety net.</li><li>The Martingale's appeal — chase a loss, recover it with one win — is really a bankroll-ruin strategy dressed up as a system.</li><li>Kelly's more conservative, edge-proportional sizing is the one actually built to survive a losing streak.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you want one book covering multiple forms of gambling rather than sports betting in isolation.</p>
