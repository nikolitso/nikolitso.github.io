<p>Ben Meer, who writes the System Sunday newsletter, starts from a simple observation: the people who keep getting things done don't have more willpower or more hours than everyone else. They have better systems. Discipline runs out and motivation comes and goes. A good system keeps working on the days you don't feel like it, and it takes a lot of the stress out of your day as well.</p>

<p>The core of the book is DRIVE, a five-step method for turning a goal into a system you can actually run. Meer then applies it across seven "Life Engines": health, relationships, career, money, emotions, mind and environment. Along the way there are more than 40 ready-made systems, including the weekly System Sunday review, plus short case studies of how people like Jerry Seinfeld, Serena Williams, Hans Zimmer and Andy Warhol organised their work.</p>

<p>This is the same idea as James Clear's "you fall to the level of your systems", but taken beyond habits to cover every part of a life. Some of it will feel familiar if you read a lot in this area. Its strength is that it is practical: you can pick one system and start using it this week.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>Lasting results come from systems, not from extra willpower or extra time.</li><li>DRIVE is a five-step method for turning any goal into a system that runs on its own.</li><li>Your life runs on seven engines (health, relationships, career, money, emotions, mind, environment), and each one needs its own systems.</li><li>A weekly review, "System Sunday", keeps the whole setup in shape.</li><li>Good systems should make life calmer and easier, not just more productive.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you set goals that fade after a few weeks and want a practical toolkit for building routines that last, rather than another pep talk about discipline.</p>
