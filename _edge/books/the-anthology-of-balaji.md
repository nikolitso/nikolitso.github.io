<p>Jorgenson compiles Balaji Srinivasan's essays, tweets, and podcast appearances into a single career-spanning collection built around three pillars: how technology is reshaping the world, how to think clearly under a flood of bad information, and how to actually go build something instead of just commentating.</p>

<p>It follows a similar format to Jorgenson's other author-anthology projects — extracted, arranged, and lightly annotated source material rather than a single connected argument — pulled from over a decade of Srinivasan's writing on startups, media, decentralization, and institutional trust.</p>

<p>The "Truth" section is the most conceptually ambitious of the three — rather than treating truth as singular, Srinivasan distinguishes between scientific, technical, political, economic, and cryptographic truth, arguing that blockchain's verifiable consensus is a genuinely new category that doesn't depend on trusting an intermediary the way the others do. The "Building" section is where the anthology is most quotable, organized around a recurring imperative — "Don't argue. Build." — that treats a working product as a more persuasive argument than any amount of debate.</p>

<p>The fragmented, quote-anthology format means depth varies wildly page to page, and Srinivasan's more polarizing political and institutional claims arrive here without much pushback or context, since the book is curated by an admirer rather than a critic.</p>

<p>The three-pillar structure holds up better than the fragmented format suggests, mostly because Jorgenson's editing keeps returning to a single throughline — that institutional trust is collapsing faster than most people have priced in, and the response should be building parallel systems rather than trying to reform the old ones from within. Whether that diagnosis is right is a separate question from whether it's argued well, and the anthology format makes it hard to press on the weaker claims.</p>
