<p>Edited by Bruce Millington, this entry in the Racing Post "Definitive Guide" series broadens out from horse racing to sports betting generally, with contributors covering how to approach markets across football, tennis, and other major sports.</p>

<p>As an edited collection it functions as a survey of practical betting knowledge — reading odds, understanding different bet types, basic bankroll thinking — rather than a single author's developed philosophy.</p>

<p>Breadth comes at the cost of depth here; if you've already read a market-structure or modeling-focused book, this will feel more introductory than revelatory.</p>

<p>The multi-sport, multi-contributor structure is genuinely useful for exactly one purpose — giving a newcomer a fast, comparative sense of how betting conventions differ across football, tennis, and racing before specializing in one, since a reader who's only ever bet on one sport tends to assume its conventions (how odds are quoted, what markets exist) are universal when they're often quite sport-specific.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>This Racing Post entry broadens out from horse racing to sports betting generally, with contributors covering football, tennis, and other major sports.</li><li>Betting conventions vary more across sports than most single-sport bettors realize — worth seeing the comparison before you specialize.</li><li>As an edited collection, it functions as a survey of practical betting knowledge rather than a single author's developed philosophy.</li><li>Breadth comes at the cost of depth — if you've already read a market-structure or modeling-focused book, this will feel more introductory.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you want one book that touches on betting across multiple sports rather than one that goes deep on a single method or sport.</p>
