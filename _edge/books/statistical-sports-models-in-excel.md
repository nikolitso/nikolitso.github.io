<p>The sequel to Mack's original Excel-modeling crash course, extending the same accessible, spreadsheet-based approach that made the first volume well-liked by readers without a statistics background.</p>

<p>Specific new content beyond “volume two” is genuinely hard to verify independently — Mack doesn't have a large public reviewer base breaking down what's different here versus the original — so it's fairest to describe this as presumably building on the same team-level, regression-based modeling approach across more sports or more advanced techniques.</p>

<p>If you got value out of the first volume's practical, build-it-yourself style, this is the natural next step; if you haven't read Volume 1, that's the better starting point regardless.</p>

<p>Given how thin the independent coverage is on this specific volume, the fairest thing is to judge it by the standard the first book set: whether the spreadsheets actually work when you build them yourself and whether the explanations hold up when you try to extend them to a new sport or season, rather than by any marketing claim about what's new in the sequel.</p>
