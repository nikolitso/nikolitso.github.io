<p>Dean Oliver's earlier work gave the sport a working vocabulary for measuring what actually wins games — possessions, four factors, individual efficiency. Twenty years and a full tracking-data revolution later, Oliver returns to ask how much of that original framework still holds, and what basketball analytics missed on the way from box scores to spatial tracking.</p>

<p>This isn't a beginner's primer — it's Oliver auditing his own legacy. He walks through how front offices actually use the numbers now, where advanced metrics have overreached, and where the simple stuff (shot selection, rebounding, turnovers) still decides games in ways tracking cameras can't fully explain. It reads as part memoir of building a field from scratch, part correction of the hype that followed.</p>

<p>The tradeoff is accessibility. Readers who don't have a baseline comfort with efficiency stats will find some chapters assume that vocabulary rather than rebuild it. It's a book for people already inside the conversation, not an entry point to it.</p>

<p>Oliver is unusually candid about where his own original four factors framework has aged less well — possession-based efficiency metrics say less than they used to now that tracking data can isolate shot quality, defensive matchups, and shot creation directly. The book reads best as a working analyst's honest accounting of which of his own ideas held up and which got superseded by better data.</p>

<p><strong>Key ideas:</strong></p>

<ul><li>The four factors were a starting vocabulary for basketball analytics, not a finished one.</li><li>Possession-based efficiency metrics say less than they used to now that tracking data can isolate shot quality and defensive matchups directly.</li><li>Oliver is candid about which of his own original ideas held up and which got superseded by better data.</li><li>The book reads best as a working analyst's honest accounting of a field's evolution, not a beginner's primer.</li></ul>

<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you've read the original and want the sequel's honest reckoning with what analytics got right and wrong the second time around.</p>
